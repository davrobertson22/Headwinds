# Discord post — Demand model 2.5 (2026-07-31)

One post, ~1,900 chars. Old Metal / restricted worlds only. Matches the register

**Demand model 2.5 — live in Old Metal**

Two changes today, both aimed at the same thing: the game let a well-run airline bank every seat, every week, forever — and no real airline gets to do that.

**Load factors are real now.** Until today, any route with more demand than seats sat at 100.0% load factor permanently. Demand doesn't arrive like that: Friday sells out while Tuesday doesn't, outbound and return never balance, and no amount of demand fixes it. From this update:

- Only ~95% of your weekly seats are achievable, ever. Deeply oversubscribed routes approach it; nothing reaches it.
- A route with demand roughly equal to its seats boards ~87% — the surplus was on the wrong days.
- Load factors breathe up to ±2.5% week to week, per route. Two identical weeks will no longer post identical numbers.
- Quiet routes are untouched. At 60% load you lose under half a point.

The read: if your network pinned at 100%, expect revenue down 5-7% and load factors that finally look like an airline's. If you're a startup filling half your seats, nothing changed.

**100 block hours.** The weekly scheduling cap drops from 140 hours to 100 — 14 hours a day is still a hard-run airline; 20 was fantasy. Existing schedules are fully grandfathered: nothing you fly today is cancelled or trimmed. But an over-cap aircraft can't take on more, and its frequency only moves one way — down. New schedules fit inside 100.

Together with the wage seniority scale that shipped alongside, this is the arc for restricted worlds: a startup's economics are untouched, and a mature airline earns a real margin instead of an absurd one.

Classic worlds are untouched, as ever. Tell me how it feels — especially anyone who was flying full: your dashboards will look different this week, and I want to know if the weekly wobble reads as alive or as noise.
