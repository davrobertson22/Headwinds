**Wi-Fi is now something you fit to an aeroplane, and a lounge is a room you build**

Two products on the Ancillaries tab have been quietly lying to you. Ticking "offered" on Wi-Fi gave your entire fleet connectivity that afternoon, with no hardware anywhere. Ticking "offered" on Lounge Passes sold day passes into a lounge you had never built, at an airport you might not even fly to. Both were switches standing in for capital nobody had spent.

**Wi-Fi** is now fitted per aircraft. $750,000 on the order form next to the engine choice, or $1,050,000 to retrofit one already in your fleet — that gap is the point of deciding at order time. It then costs $1,500 a week per fitted aeroplane whether it flies or sits on a reserve stand, because the airtime contract is bought by the airframe. You still set the price once for the whole airline; what's changed is that a route flown by an unfitted jet sells no Wi-Fi and takes the penalty for not having it. The Fleet page will retrofit a whole selection in one go.

**Lounges** cost $12,000,000, take ten weeks to fit out and $85,000 a week to run, and you can build one at any airport where you hold a gate — hub or not. A lounge lifts your pull with *business* travellers on every route touching it, and does nothing at all for leisure passengers, who can't get in. The money comes back on the ground bill: you've always paid $60 a business passenger and $110 a first passenger for lounge access and premium handling, and at an airport you own that drops to about a third. Which means a lounge earns at a hub pushing real premium volume and slowly bleeds at a thin outstation. You choose whether loyalty members and alliance partners' members get in free.

**What it costs you right now:** nothing in your existing fleet is fitted. There was no honest way to hand every save a fleet full of hardware it never bought. So every aeroplane you own is now an aeroplane without Wi-Fi, taking the penalty for it, and Lounge Passes earn nothing until you've built somewhere. That's roughly four quality points off your routes until you spend the money. It's a real hit, it's deliberate, and the whole point of the change is that these things now cost something.

Whether a lounge is worth building is a genuine question — $85,000 a week is a lot of route profit. Tell me if the numbers are wrong.
