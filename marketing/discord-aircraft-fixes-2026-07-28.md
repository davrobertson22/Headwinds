# Discord post — aircraft data fixes (2026-07-28)

Reply to Kat the Fox's three market-card reports. One post, ~1,500 chars.

**Aircraft data pass — thanks Kat, you found three real bugs**

The 747-8I looking smaller than the -400 isn't the bug. Those seat numbers are Boeing's actual certified exit limits: the -400 is placarded for 660, and Boeing never re-certified the -8I's limit despite the 5.6 m stretch, so it sits at 605. Same reason the -300 matches the -400 — they share a fuselage and the same stretched upper deck. Those numbers stay, but the market cards now explain themselves instead of just looking broken.

What *was* broken is what sat next to them. Two aircraft had their fuel burn entered at about 65% of the real figure while all 161 others sat at 92-98%. The A380 was showing 1.40 L/seat/100km — better than a 787-9 — and the 747-8I was scoring 98/100 on efficiency. Fixed: A380 1197 → 1800, 747-8I 986 → 1400. The -8I is still the more efficient 747, just by a believable margin now.

Same class of error in cargo: several freighters were burning less than the identical passenger airframe they're converted from. The MD-11F was 27% thriftier than an MD-11. All corrected.

And yes, the ATR 72-600F was overpriced — $26M against $22M for the passenger -600, same airframe with a freight door. Now $22M, lease $52k → $44k.

Last one: 40-year-old freighter conversions were cheaper to maintain per tonne than brand-new ones, so there was never a reason to buy new. The classics keep their cheap lease and cheap purchase — they now cost what an old airframe actually costs to keep flying.

**Heads up:** if you fly A380s, 747-8s, 747-400Fs or classic freighters, your costs go up from the next tick. That's the correction, not a nerf.
