**Demand model 3.0 — cities, not airports**

The biggest known exploit in the demand model is gone. Until today, every airport in a multi-airport city carried the *whole* city's demand: JFK–Heathrow, Newark–Heathrow and LaGuardia–Stansted were each a full-size New York–London market. Fly two of them and you'd sold the same city twice — measured at ~2x, and Tokyo–Osaka could hit 4x. The route map said your loads were honest; the world was printing duplicate passengers underneath.

From this tick, a city pair is **one market**. Around 35 metros are grouped — New York, London, Tokyo, Seoul, Paris, Moscow, São Paulo, Shanghai, the lot — and every route between two metros fights in the same pool: your routes, AI carriers, and every other player, whichever member airports you each fly from. Your JFK service and your EWR service now compete with each other, and a rival undercutting you out of Gatwick takes passengers off your Heathrow run.

**Airports have missions now.** Each member airport has a haul-aware appeal. LaGuardia is a first-class choice for domestic short-haul and nearly worthless past its perimeter; City Airport can't do long-haul; Itami, Gimpo, Songshan and Congonhas are domestic machines; a lone route from Newburgh cannot vacuum up the New York market no matter how cheap it is. Right airport, right mission — it matters for the first time.

**Demand grows as your world ages.** Air travel demand rises fastest where incomes are climbing — India and Southeast Asia lead, the US and Japan crawl — and the growth is deliberately gradual: an emerging-market route gains roughly +15–20% by year five and about doubles over a full century; mature markets add about a quarter in a hundred years. Planting a flag early in an emerging market pays off across a world's whole life — it won't flip the map in a decade.

Also fixed: pairs of different cities that share a name (the two Columbuses, Norfolks, Albanys…) wrongly had *zero* demand. They fly now.

The read: if you were stacking multiple airport pairs into one big city, that revenue corrects this week — it was never real. Single-airport-pair routes mostly hold or improve, since city totals were recalibrated against real O&D data (median within 5% across 47 benchmark pairs).
