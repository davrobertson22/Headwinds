Flagships: Double-Deckers & Supersonic

The A380, 747 family and Concorde in Tailwinds: seats, range, lease cost and economics of the game’s biggest and fastest aircraft — and when they actually pay.

By the Tailwinds team · Last updated October 29, 2026

These are the trophy aircraft of Tailwinds — the double-deck giants and the supersonic Concorde. Every player wants to fly them. Most players lose money the first time they try. This page is about flying them profitably.

Double-deckers live or die on one question: can the route fill 400–550 seats week after week? That only happens on the world's great trunk routes, usually between mega-hubs with strong feed on both ends. When it works, per-seat costs are excellent. When it doesn't, the weekly lease alone erases the profit of half your network.

Concorde is its own economy. Its fuel burn is extraordinary, but supersonic prestige commands a large ticket premium in-game, and on the right premium transatlantic-style pairing it can work as a halo aircraft — brand first, margin second.

How to choose

Honestly? Wait. Flagships belong in a mature network with established mega-hubs, deep cash reserves, and proven trunk routes bursting past the capacity of your largest twins. Then, and only then, they are glorious.

What it costs to hold, and to buy

Type-by-type notes

Concorde

Aérospatiale/BAC · Supersonic

Seats 128 Range 7,250 km at 100 seats (7,000 km at 128) Cruise 2,180 km/h Lease $463K/wk Buy $190M Fuel 1491 L/100km (≈ $21.61/km) Maintenance $320K/wk

The supersonic icon. Mach 2 across the Atlantic in under 3.5 hours. Charges 2.5× fares but burns fuel like nothing else. Prestige over profit.

Fuel efficiency: #9 of 9 in its class (16.88¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — toward the thirstier end per seat. Weekly lease works out to $3,617 per seat.

Boeing 747SP

Boeing · Double Deck

Seats 400 Range 10,800 km at 276 seats (9,600 km at 400) Cruise 870 km/h Lease $43K/wk Buy $22M Fuel 1338 L/100km (≈ $19.40/km) Maintenance $265K/wk

The shortened long-ranger. Fewer seats than a standard 747 but extraordinary range. The first aircraft to operate true nonstop ultra-long-haul polar routes.

Fuel efficiency: #8 of 9 in its class (4.85¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — toward the thirstier end per seat. Weekly lease works out to $108 per seat.

Boeing 747-200B

Boeing · Double Deck

Seats 550 Range 12,700 km at 366 seats (11,400 km at 550) Cruise 870 km/h Lease $40K/wk Buy $20M Fuel 1725 L/100km (≈ $25.01/km) Maintenance $275K/wk

Classic first-generation jumbo. Very cheap to buy but extremely thirsty.

Fuel efficiency: #6 of 9 in its class (4.55¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — mid-pack on per-seat fuel efficiency. Weekly lease works out to $73 per seat.

Boeing 747-100

Boeing · Double Deck

Seats 550 Range 9,800 km at 366 seats (8,500 km at 550) Cruise 870 km/h Lease $31K/wk Buy $16M Fuel 1819 L/100km (≈ $26.37/km) Maintenance $270K/wk

The original Jumbo Jet that democratized long-haul travel in 1970.

Fuel efficiency: #7 of 9 in its class (4.79¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — toward the thirstier end per seat. Weekly lease works out to $56 per seat.

Boeing 747-400

Boeing · Double Deck

Seats 605 Range 13,445 km at 416 seats (12,000 km at 605) Cruise 870 km/h Lease $109K/wk Buy $55M Fuel 1608 L/100km (≈ $23.32/km) Maintenance $310K/wk

The Queen of the Skies. Placarded at the standard 605-seat 747 exit limit, cheap to acquire and able to reach almost anywhere — but four thirsty engines make it a high-density trunk aircraft rather than an efficient one.

Fuel efficiency: #4 of 9 in its class (3.85¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — mid-pack on per-seat fuel efficiency. Weekly lease works out to $180 per seat.

Boeing 747-8I

Boeing · Double Deck

Seats 605 Range 14,816 km at 467 seats (13,600 km at 605) Cruise 870 km/h Lease $186K/wk Buy $80M Fuel 1400 L/100km (≈ $20.30/km) Maintenance $340K/wk

Latest and longest 747 — but Boeing never re-certified the exit limit, so 5.6 m of extra fuselage buys not one seat over the -400. What it buys instead: a fifth less fuel per seat and 1,400 km more range.

Fuel efficiency: #2 of 9 in its class (3.36¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — among the most fuel-efficient per seat. Weekly lease works out to $307 per seat.

Boeing 747-300

Boeing · Double Deck

Seats 605 Range 11,700 km at 400 seats (10,300 km at 605) Cruise 870 km/h Lease $64K/wk Buy $32M Fuel 1776 L/100km (≈ $25.75/km) Maintenance $275K/wk

The stretched-upper-deck 747. Shares the -400 cabin and its 605-seat exit limit for a fraction of the price — older engines make it far thirstier and it gives up 1,700 km of range.

Fuel efficiency: #5 of 9 in its class (4.26¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — mid-pack on per-seat fuel efficiency. Weekly lease works out to $106 per seat.

Boeing 747-400D

Boeing · Double Deck

Seats 660 Range 3,700 km at 568 seats (3,000 km at 660) Cruise 870 km/h Lease $109K/wk Buy $55M Fuel 1608 L/100km (≈ $23.32/km) Maintenance $310K/wk

The domestic Jumbo. Built for high-frequency short sectors: strengthened for the cycles, stripped of its winglets and flown at a reduced max weight, which cuts its range to 3,700 km. In exchange it is the only 747 ever certified for the full 660-seat exit limit — 55 more than any other. Same price, same burn, same cabin as the -400; you are trading range for seats and nothing else.

Fuel efficiency: #3 of 9 in its class (3.53¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — among the most fuel-efficient per seat. Weekly lease works out to $165 per seat.

Airbus A380

Airbus · Double Deck

Seats 853 Range 15,200 km at 525 seats (12,900 km at 853) Cruise 870 km/h Lease $740K/wk Buy $305M Fuel 1800 L/100km (≈ $26.10/km) Maintenance $390K/wk

The double-decker superjumbo. Unmatched capacity on ultra-high-demand routes — and priced for it: the most airframe money can buy.

Fuel efficiency: #1 of 9 in its class (3.06¢ per seat-km at base fuel price) — among the most fuel-efficient per seat. Weekly lease works out to $868 per seat.
